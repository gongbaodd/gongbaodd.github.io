# Week 51: Building an Interactive Novel with Pannellum and Inky 

This past week, I had the privilege of attending the **Interactive Environments for Virtualization of Cultural Heritage** [Erasmus project](https://www.tlu.ee/en/dt/news/students-germany-and-cyprus-joined-tlu-international-project-week). 🇪🇪 During this intensive week, our team focused on creating an interactive novel based on the historical **WWII Tannenberg Line Battle**.

### 🎮 The Project: An Interactive Historical Experience

You can play the final game prototype right here: [**https://gongbaodd.itch.io/1944**](https://gongbaodd.itch.io/1944)

Our workflow involved several key tools to bring this narrative to life:

1.  **Narrative Logic (Inky):** We initially used [**Inky**](https://www.inklestudios.com/ink/) to quickly build a low-fidelity prototype. This allowed us to finalize the game's core routine and player decision pathways efficiently.
2.  **3D Presentation (Pannellum & AI):** Once the narrative structure was solid, we leveraged [**Pannellum**](https://pannellum.org/) to create the immersive 3D panorama slideshow. The visuals for these panoramas were generated with the help of **Doubao AI** ([**https://www.doubao.com/chat/**](https://www.doubao.com/chat/)).

### 🔬 User Testing and Key Findings

A crucial part of our project involved user testing to evaluate the experience. We used **iMotions** ([**https://imotions.com/**](https://imotions.com/)) to track and analyze users' emotional responses while they played the game.

I was particularly excited to see that the motivations of some testers clearly reflected aspects of the [**Bartle taxonomy of player types**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartle_taxonomy_of_player_types). This provided valuable insight into how different players engage with the narrative.

![Data analysis](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmq8ipket/image/upload/v1765719501/Screenshot_2025-12-14_153743_uk2i6n.png)

Here are a few other significant findings from our testing:


* **Complexity Requires Guidance:** For games with complex interactions (like a draggable panorama background), a dedicated **tutorial is essential**. Several testers were unaware that they could manipulate the background.
* **The Hawthorne Effect in Testing:** Testers under surveillance (knowing they are being watched or tracked) often exhibit "safer" interaction behaviors, such as strictly clicking intended buttons. For example, a hidden "doodle feature" was only used when testers were explicitly asked to try it, rather than spontaneously exploring it—a behavior that contradicts the natural curiosity players typically exhibit in a less monitored environment.
