# Week 9: The IKEA Effect

This week, I hit up two events in Tallinn: [Gamedev Guild](https://communities.unrealengine.com/events/details/epic-games-tallinn-presents-gamedev-guild-v214-feat-unreal-tallinn/) and the [Vibe Code Atelier Tallinn](https://luma.com/ah0iu2db).

### Bilt.me 📱

During the Vibe Coding event, the team from [Bilt.me](https://bilt.me/) showcased their project—a vibe-coding service using Expo to spin up mobile apps. They were a bit vague on the technical details, but honestly, building a service like this isn’t "hard" if you have the budget to burn on LLM tokens. 💸

I think the real engineering job here is just babysitting token usage, likely using something like **LangChain** or **Langfuse**. It’s the same vibe as [bolt.new](https://bolt.new) or [Lovable](https://lovable.dev), but this one doesn’t actually give you the code. I guess they don’t want users touching the codebase. 🛑💻

### The "AI Baby" Syndrome 👶🤖

The showcase felt like a massive **IKEA effect** experiment. People are using AI to build products and treating the output like their own child—they love it because they "built" it, even if the AI did the heavy lifting.

A few observations from the crowd:

* **The Bar is Low:** There were so many "unprofessional" comments. It’s the 21st century—why are people still acting shocked that a website can record your voice and transpile it to text? 🎙️
* **Wait until they see the real stuff:** If they’re terrified by basic features, imagine their reaction to AI actually controlling a computer like **Manus** or **Openclaw** (even if those are pricey to run). 🤖💻

### The Silver Lining 🤑

In the end, it’s actually a good thing for us. These folks are well-connected and, frankly, pretty rich. They’ll use their "loved" AI-generated babies to secure big investments... and then they’ll have to hire people like me to actually fix the bugs and make it work. 📈🛠️

What a future.
